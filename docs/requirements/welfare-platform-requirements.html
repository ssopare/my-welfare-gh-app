01Introduction

Purpose & scope

This document translates a plain-language product brief and four real welfare constitutions into an implementation-ready requirements specification for a multi-tenant welfare management mobile platform.

The brief asks for a single mobile application that thousands of independent welfare groups — university staff associations, ministry welfare schemes, private-school associations, alumni unions — can each download, register on, and run autonomously, with financial behaviour driven entirely by configuration rather than by per-client code. To ground that ambition in something other than abstraction, four constitutions were supplied as source material and are treated throughout this document as representative case law:

These four differ in almost every financial particular — contribution amount, cadence, collection mechanism, benefit catalogue, payout amount, eligibility waiting period, and even in how consistently they were amended — while sharing an identical shape: a pool, a set of trigger events, a set of payouts, an approval chain, and a way to lose eligibility. That shape is the product's real subject matter. Section 3 makes the comparison explicit; Sections 9–11 show how it becomes configuration rather than code.

1.1What this document is, and isn't

This is a Business & Product Requirements Specification (BRD/PRD hybrid): it defines what the system must do, the rules it must be able to express, and the architecture qualities it must have. It stops short of API contracts, screen-by-screen UI specs, and sprint planning — those belong to the technical design and delivery documents this specification feeds.

03Design philosophy

Constitution-as-configuration

The single hardest requirement in the brief is also the one every naive welfare app gets wrong: financial logic must be data, not deployment.

3.1What every constitution has, structurally

  • A membership lifecycle — join (with or without probation/grace period), remain in good standing, default, exit (voluntary, involuntary, or by death), and sometimes re-enter.
  • A funding side — one or more contribution obligations, each with an amount, a cadence, a collection mechanism, and a review procedure.
  • A benefit catalogue — trigger events (birth, marriage, bereavement of a defined relative, illness, resignation, retirement) each mapped to a payout, a frequency cap, and a verification requirement.
  • An eligibility gate — minimum tenure, good standing, and (in every source document) some rule that removes eligibility on default or on early exit.
  • An approval chain — someone verifies, someone recommends, someone approves, someone disburses, and someone reports back to the membership.
  • A governance body that can itself change all of the above, subject to a quorum and a majority threshold.

3.2What varies — and why hardcoding it fails

Every numeric value, cadence, relationship definition, and threshold above varies by organisation, and several vary within a single organisation's own lifetime — the AGAOSAS constitution is, in the source document, visibly mid-amendment: its Article 8 and Article 10 each carry two contradictory generations of benefit figures (a flat GHC 1,000/500 regime being superseded by a GHC 3,000/2,500 regime) sitting in the same file. A platform that encodes "bereavement payout" as a constant, or as a value tied to a specific benefit-type enum, cannot survive its first client's first amendment, let alone its second client's entirely different benefit catalogue (the Ministry of Works pays a benefit for the employee's own death; UDS instead treats an employee's death as a payment to survivors — these are not the same rule with a different number, they are structurally different claims).

Design principle

Nothing that appears as a number, a name, a time period, or a relationship in a constitution may be a constant in code. It must be a row in a rules table, versioned, effective-dated, and editable by an authorised role inside the product — the same product members use to pay dues and file claims.

3.3Consequence for architecture

This pushes the platform toward a rule-engine-first architecture (Section 11): a small, stable core (tenant, member, ledger, transaction, claim, approval) surrounded by a configuration layer that every tenant's onboarding wizard populates and every tenant's admins can subsequently amend, with full version history — because "the constitution changed" must be an audit event, not a support ticket.

What this comparison forces into the data model

Four independent, non-communicating organisations converged on the same primitives — waiting period, good-standing test, default consequence, verification gate — while disagreeing on every threshold and every amount, and one of them (AGAOSAS) shows that even a single organisation's own thresholds move over time. That is direct evidence for a rule engine with effective-dated, tenant-scoped parameters rather than a benefit-type enum with a price column.

07System overview

Tenancy model & onboarding flow

Four levels, matching the UDS precedent of a national association with independent campus chapters — the deepest structure in the source set, and the one every shallower structure fits inside as a degenerate case.

Platform
One physical deployment. Owns global auth, billing, abuse monitoring, and the master rule-engine schema every tenant configuration validates against.
Organisation
A tenant: "UDS Staff Welfare Association", "AGAOSAS". Owns its constitution config, its member roster, its funds, its brand (logo/colour on their instance of the app).
Group / Chapter
Optional sub-unit — a campus, a branch office, a class-year cohort. Inherits the parent organisation's rules unless explicitly overridden (e.g. a campus-level imprest allocation); has its own convener role and its own claim-escalation path.
Member
A person, with exactly one membership status per organisation, optionally holding one or more role assignments (RBAC, Section 13).
Identity vs. membership

A person routinely belongs to more than one of these organisations at once — the same individual might be in a university staff association and an alumni union and a hometown welfare group. The tenant-isolation model in Section 21 correctly keeps their financial standing, dues, and claims completely separate in each — that boundary must never be weakened. But the login is a different layer from the membership: one phone-number-based Account can hold several Memberships, one per organisation, the same way a single Google account can belong to several Workspaces. The app presents a lightweight switcher between memberships; whichever one is active is always visible on screen, so a member is never confused about which group's dues they're paying or which group's balance they're viewing. See Section 10 for how Account and Member are modelled as distinct entities.

7.1Onboarding flow

  1. Register organisation — legal name, type (employer-linked / voluntary), country & currency, admin identity verification.
  2. Constitution wizard — a guided, form-driven interview (not a document upload parser) that walks the founding admin through: governance tiers, contribution plan(s), benefit catalogue, eligibility rules, defaulter policy, and approval chains. Every screen maps 1:1 to a table in Section 8.
  3. Optional: attach source document — the actual constitution PDF/Word file may be uploaded for reference and audit purposes; it is never parsed as the source of truth, only linked to the resulting configuration for human cross-checking.
  4. Role assignment — invite officers by phone/email; assign role templates or custom roles.
  5. Member import / self-registration — bulk CSV import (for payroll-linked employers) or open self-registration with admin approval (for voluntary associations).
  6. Go-live review — a plain-language summary ("Your members will pay GHS 20/month by mobile money; a bereaved member's spouse's death pays out GHS 500 after verification…") for the admin to confirm before the configuration becomes live and billable.
Decision made

Chapters (Tier 3) are modelled in the schema from day one, but the UI for managing them — and the imprest sub-fund and escalation-SLA machinery that goes with it — is feature-flagged behind the Federation module, bundled into the Enterprise subscription tier (Section 18.4). A single-site tenant never sees a chapter screen; a multi-campus tenant like UDS turns it on as part of the tier it lands in anyway on member count. This resolves what was previously an open question without adding a separate pricing axis or a data-model rewrite later.

7.2End-to-end registration flow

Section 7.1 covers an organisation onboarding itself; this walks through what happens for a person, first joining one group and then joining a second — a sequence that touches onboarding, membership, and the Account/Membership split above, but hadn't previously been written out as one continuous path.

Joining the first group. A prospective member arrives via an admin's invite link/QR code (voluntary orgs) or is bulk-imported from a payroll list (employer-linked orgs). If they've never used the platform before, registering creates their Account (phone number verification) at the same moment it creates their first Membership. They complete personal particulars and pre-register dependants/beneficiaries (FR-MEM-03) — this matters because a bereavement claim later is checked against names entered before the event, never added retroactively. The system places them in that org's configured initial state (Pending, Probation, or Active, per FR-MEM-01) and starts the tenure clock and the four grace/waiting-period timers (Section 12.4) from whichever date that org's own rules specify.

Joining a second, unrelated group. The same person follows a second org's own invite link, or taps "Join another group" inside the app. Because their Account already exists, there's no repeat phone-number verification — the flow only creates a new Membership, scoped entirely to the new org: its own tenure clock, its own contribution schedule, its own approval chains. Dependants are prefilled from an existing membership as a convenience, but never carried over as already-verified — each org has its own standard for what counts as a "recognised" spouse or child, so the member must explicitly re-confirm each dependant before it counts as pre-registered for that org's claims. The workspace switcher (Section 7) now shows both memberships; only one is ever active on screen.

FR-MEM-09

Registering into an organisation for the first time shall create an Account only if the person does not already hold one; an existing Account shall gain an additional Membership without repeating identity verification.

FR-MEM-10

When a member with an existing Membership elsewhere registers a new Membership, the system may prefill dependant/beneficiary records from the existing Membership as a data-entry convenience, but each prefilled dependant shall require explicit re-confirmation before it is treated as pre-registered for that organisation's own claims.

08Functional requirements

What the system must do

Grouped by module. Each requirement is numbered FR-<module>-<n> for traceability into test plans.

8.1Tenant & organisation onboarding

FR-ONB-01

A prospective organisation shall be able to self-register a tenant from the mobile app without platform-owner intervention, subject to identity verification of the founding admin.

FR-ONB-02

The system shall provide a guided constitution wizard covering governance, contributions, benefits, eligibility, and defaulter policy, producing a structured configuration object rather than free text.

FR-ONB-03

The system shall allow attaching reference documents (the organisation's actual constitution) to a tenant without treating them as an executable rule source.

FR-ONB-04

The system shall support cloning an existing tenant's configuration as a starting template for a new tenant (e.g. "start from a university staff-welfare template").

FR-ONB-05

The system shall present a plain-language, member-facing summary of the configured rules for admin sign-off before activation.

FR-ONB-06

Where an uploaded source document contains internally contradictory figures for the same rule (e.g. a due stated as both "Ten Ghana Cedis" and "GHc 5.00" in the same clause — a real defect found in one of the four source constitutions), the wizard shall surface the contradiction to the admin as a warning requiring an explicit choice, and shall never silently resolve it to either value.

8.2Membership management

FR-MEM-01

The system shall support member lifecycle states: Pending, Probation, Active (good standing), Grace, Defaulter, Suspended, Exited, Deceased, each configurable per tenant as to entry/exit conditions.

FR-MEM-02

The system shall enforce a configurable waiting period (e.g. UDS's 6 months, AGAOSAS's 3-month probation) before a member becomes eligible for any benefit, independent of contribution status.

FR-MEM-03

The system shall support member-declared dependants/beneficiaries (spouse, children, parents) as first-class records, pre-registered and time-stamped, mirroring St. Peter's and MoWH's "personal particulars form" requirement, so that a bereavement claim can be validated against a name registered before the event.

FR-MEM-04

The system shall support member transfer between chapters of the same organisation without loss of tenure or contribution history.

FR-MEM-05

The system shall support voluntary exit with a configurable settlement rule (e.g. 1/3 refund, 75% refund, forfeiture) computed automatically from contribution history and benefit-claim history.

FR-MEM-06

The system shall support a configurable re-entry policy for previously exited members (AGAOSAS: one chance, arrears + fresh probation).

FR-MEM-07

The system shall distinguish membership categories ineligible for contribution/benefit (e.g. UDS contract staff) from the standard category, without requiring a separate tenant.

FR-MEM-08

The system shall model a person's login identity (Account) as distinct from any single organisation's membership record (Member), so one account can hold multiple memberships across different tenants, switchable in-app, without weakening the tenant-isolation guarantees in Section 21 or merging financial standing between organisations.

8.3Governance & committee configuration

FR-GOV-01

The system shall allow a tenant to define an arbitrary number of governance bodies (assembly, council, committees), each with a name, membership composition rule, quorum, tie-break rule, and meeting cadence.

FR-GOV-02

The system shall support term limits and cooling-off periods for officer roles, and shall auto-flag a role as vacant when its holder's membership status changes to Exited or Deceased.

FR-GOV-03

The system shall support recording motions, minutes, and votes for any configured meeting, with a configurable notice period and quorum check before a vote can be recorded as valid.

FR-GOV-04

The system shall support a "vote of no confidence" workflow that reassigns a role to a designated successor (e.g. Vice-Chair) pending a fresh election.

8.4Financial rule engine

FR-RULE-01

The system shall represent every contribution obligation, benefit entitlement, eligibility gate, and penalty as a versioned, effective-dated configuration record scoped to a tenant (and optionally a chapter).

FR-RULE-02

The system shall never delete a rule version; amendments create a new version with an effective date, and historical transactions remain evaluated against the rule version in force at the time.

FR-RULE-03

The system shall support rule amendment via the same governance workflow the tenant configured (Section 8.3) — e.g. requiring committee recommendation before a Chair or Assembly can activate a new rule version.

FR-RULE-04

The system shall support per-relative-type benefit definitions (spouse, child, parent, self/member) with independent amounts and independent occurrence caps (e.g. "child death, cap 3", "parent death, cap 1 per parent").

8.5Contributions & ledger

FR-LED-01

The system shall support multiple concurrent contribution plans per tenant (e.g. a monthly due plus a one-time registration fee plus event-triggered levies, as in AGAOSAS).

FR-LED-02

The system shall support both push collection (member-initiated payment) and pull collection (payroll deduction file export/import) as configurable per-plan mechanisms.

FR-LED-03

The system shall support advance payment of future contribution periods, crediting the member's standing forward and displaying a "paid through" date.

FR-LED-04

The system shall reconcile payroll-file remittances against expected per-member obligations and surface variances (short-paid, unremitted, over-paid) to the Treasurer role.

FR-LED-05

The system shall maintain an immutable, append-only transaction ledger per fund, from which all statements and reports are derived (never mutate a posted transaction; corrections are reversing entries).

FR-LED-06

The system shall support ring-fenced sub-funds where a constitution requires it (e.g. a separate imprest float per campus/chapter, per UDS).

FR-LED-07

The system shall support a member wallet: a stored-value balance that can be topped up and applied toward any of that member's obligations (not pre-committed to one specific contribution period the way advance payment in FR-LED-03 is), modelled as a liability account per member on the tenant's ledger (Section 12).

FR-LED-08

Member wallet balances shall be one-directional: top-up and apply-to-obligation only. The system shall not support cash withdrawal from a wallet or member-to-member wallet transfer — a wallet's only paths out are settling an obligation or the standard exit-refund mechanism (FR-MEM-05) — to keep the platform clearly outside e-money/mobile-money-issuer licensing territory rather than a SaaS tool managing an association's own funds.

This module's underlying accounting model — full double-entry, not just an append-only log — is specified in Section 12. Core ledger primitives (chart of accounts, journal entries, immutability, wallet-as-liability) are in scope for initial launch; advanced modules (investment tracking, budgeting, imprest automation) are Phase 2 — see Section 23.

8.6Claims & benefit disbursement

FR-CLM-01

The system shall let a member (or an admin on their behalf) file a claim against a configured benefit type, capturing the required evidence type for that benefit (e.g. death certificate, hospital admission note, marriage certificate).

FR-CLM-02

The system shall evaluate claim eligibility automatically against tenure, good-standing, occurrence-cap, and waiting-period rules, and shall block submission (with a clear reason) when a hard rule fails, while flagging soft/discretionary conditions for human review.

FR-CLM-03

The system shall route a claim through the tenant's configured approval chain (e.g. Convener verifies → Committee recommends → Treasurer disburses), recording each actor and timestamp.

FR-CLM-04

The system shall support two claim registers per constitution's own language where required (applications vs. approved-and-paid, per UDS Finance Committee duty), derivable as a report rather than a separate data store.

FR-CLM-05

The system shall support discretionary/graded benefits (e.g. MoWH's severity-graded illness subsidy, GHS 500–1,000) where an approver selects within a configured band rather than a fixed amount, with the choice recorded and reportable.

FR-CLM-06

The approval chain shall support sequential, parallel, and amount-threshold-based routing (e.g. "claims under GHS 1,000: Welfare Officer → Treasurer"; "claims over GHS 5,000: Welfare Officer → Finance Committee → Chairman → Treasurer") within a configurable in-app workflow state machine. A dedicated workflow-engine dependency (Temporal/Camunda) is not required for v1 — see Section 05.

8.7Defaulter management & anti-abuse

Detailed algorithmically in Section 14; functional requirements only, here.

FR-DEF-01

The system shall track consecutive missed-contribution counts per member per plan and transition membership status automatically per configured thresholds (AGAOSAS: 2 months → not in good standing; 3 months → forfeiture of entitlement).

FR-DEF-02

The system shall require arrears to be cleared before restoring good standing, and shall support a configurable order-of-application for part-payments against oldest arrears first (per AGAOSAS's "prior arrears before current").

FR-DEF-03

The system shall detect and flag "dumping" patterns — a member with low lifetime contribution consistency filing a claim shortly after resuming payment — for manual committee review rather than silently paying out.

FR-DEF-04

The system shall support a minimum contribution-months-before-eligible rule independent of calendar tenure, so a member who joined long ago but paid irregularly is treated differently from one who has paid consistently since joining.

8.8Payments

FR-PAY-01

The system shall support in-app contribution payment via mobile money (MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash, AirtelTigo Money), card networks, and bank transfer, brokered through a licensed payment aggregator.

FR-PAY-02

The system shall support payroll-deduction as a first-class collection channel, including generating an employer-facing deduction file and reconciling its return.

FR-PAY-05

The transaction shall record the paying identity and the obligation-holder as two distinct fields — never conflating them — so both parties' payment histories and consistency scores (Section 14.1) remain accurate.

FR-PAY-06

A member with an existing wallet balance (Section 8.5, FR-LED-07) shall be able to settle an obligation from that balance without initiating a new payment, covering the case where they currently have no funds on their payment instrument.

FR-PAY-03

The system shall support disbursement of approved benefits via mobile money or bank transfer to the beneficiary (member or named next-of-kin), with payout confirmation captured against the claim record.

FR-PAY-04

The system shall reconcile every payment provider's webhook/callback against the ledger within a bounded SLA and surface unreconciled transactions to the Treasurer.

FR-ELC-02

09Non-functional requirements

Qualities the system must have

Scale & performance

  • Support tens of thousands of tenants and low millions of members platform-wide; a single tenant may range from a 5-person alumni cohort to a multi-campus employer with tens of thousands of staff.
  • P95 API latency < 300 ms for read paths (balance, standing, statement); claim submission and payment initiation < 2 s end-to-end excluding external payment-provider round trips.
  • Ledger writes must be strictly ordered and idempotent under retry (payment webhooks routinely double-deliver).

Availability & resilience

  • Target 99.9% platform availability; graceful degradation to read-only mode during payment-provider outages rather than full outage.
  • Offline-tolerant mobile client: dues payment history, standing status, and claim drafts must be viewable/composable without connectivity, syncing on reconnect — most source constitutions serve rural/campus populations with patchy connectivity.

Security & privacy

  • Tenant data isolation must be provable, not just conventional (see Section 21).
  • PII (national ID, next-of-kin details, medical evidence) encrypted at rest, field-level for the most sensitive categories.
  • Payment credential data never touches platform servers directly — tokenised via PCI-DSS-compliant aggregator.
  • Compliance with Ghana's Data Protection Act 2012 as the primary jurisdiction evidenced by the source documents, designed extensibly for other data-protection regimes as the platform expands geographically.

Auditability

  • Every financial rule, role grant, and disbursement must be reconstructable after the fact — "what rule was in force, who approved it, why" — without relying on application logs alone.

Localisation

  • Multi-currency-ready even though GHS dominates the source set; English first, with a translation layer for future local-language support.

Extensibility

  • New benefit types, contribution mechanisms, and approval-chain shapes must be addable via configuration/admin UI, not a schema migration, for at least the patterns evidenced across the four source constitutions.

11Rule engine specification

Turning clauses into configuration

Two worked examples, taken directly from the source constitutions, showing the same schema expressing structurally different rules — framed by a pipeline that GPT's analysis names most clearly of the six.

11.1The Programmable Welfare Finance Engine pipeline

GPT's independent analysis converges on the same architecture every other source implies piecemeal, but states it as one explicit pipeline — the clearest single articulation across all six analyses, worth adopting as the vocabulary for this entire section:

Constitution → Policy → Version → Rule → Obligation → Payment → Allocation
           → Standing → Eligibility → Claim → Approval → Disbursement → Ledger

Two distinctions embedded in that pipeline are easy to collapse by accident and must not be:

  • Obligation ≠ Payment. An obligation is what a member was required to pay for a given period; a payment is money actually received. AGAOSAS's own rules depend on this split — a member can owe a January contribution, pay it in June, and the system must still know it was originally due in January and unpaid until June, not that it was "paid on time." Collapsing the two into a single "paid = yes/no" flag destroys exactly the history the anti-dumping logic in Section 14 depends on.
  • Historical fact ≠ current state. "February's contribution was due 28 February and paid 18 May" is a historical fact that must never change. "February's contribution is now settled" is a current-state summary derived from it. Reporting, auditing, and eligibility all need the fact preserved even after the state is resolved.

A related distinction the rule engine's date arithmetic must respect: event date (when the qualifying event happened — a death, a birth, a marriage), claim date (when the member submitted), approval date, payment date, and rule-effective date are five different fields, not one "date" column. Eligibility is evaluated against whichever date the tenant's rule configuration says governs — usually the event date and the rule version in force on that date — never against "whichever date happens to be convenient," which matters most exactly when a constitution changes in the gap between an event occurring and a claim eventually being paid.

GPT's analysis proposes both of the following as named capabilities; neither appears in the other five in this explicit a form, but both follow directly from a rule engine that is already declarative and versioned — the marginal cost of adding them is mostly in the UI, not the underlying model.

  • "Why am I / am I not eligible?" engine — every eligibility decision the rule engine makes must be answerable in plain language, not just true/false. FR-RULE-06

The system shall provide a sandbox in which a proposed rule version can be evaluated against hypothetical member scenarios before activation, with no effect on real accounts or ledgers.

12Ledger & accounting architecture

Full double-entry, not a payments table

Only one of the four independent analyses (Grok) specified this; this consolidated spec adopts it anyway, upgrading the "immutable transaction log" pattern the other three (including the original version of this document) settled for — because reconciliation, multi-fund allocation, and audit-defensible corrections all genuinely need it.

12.1Chart of accounts

Every Fund (Section 10) owns its own chart of accounts: Assets (cash, bank, mobile-money, gateway-clearing, settlement accounts), Liabilities (benefits payable, member advance-payment credits), Income (contributions, donations, grants, investment interest), Expenses (benefits paid, penalties waived, operating costs), and a Fund/Equity balance that is always the residual of the above — never a separately-edited number.

12.2Journal entries & multi-fund allocation

Every financial event — a contribution received, a benefit disbursed, a penalty applied, a refund issued, a transfer between funds — posts exactly one JournalEntry composed of two or more balanced JournalLines (total debits = total credits). Once posted, an entry is never edited or deleted; corrections are reversing/contra entries, fully traceable to the original.

A single contribution can be split across multiple funds by a configurable percentage allocation — e.g. GHS 100 → GHS 60 to the General Welfare Fund, GHS 25 to the Funeral Fund, GHS 15 to the Emergency Fund. Changing that split takes effect prospectively only; it must never rewrite how a historical contribution was already allocated.

12.3Reconciliation

Three reconciliation loops run continuously rather than only at audit time: payment-gateway settlement vs. internal ledger, bank statement vs. ledger, and cash collections vs. posted receipts. Each flags unmatched entries, duplicates, and amount mismatches to the Treasurer role for resolution — the system never auto-corrects a mismatch on its own.

FR-LEDGER-01

The system shall maintain a per-fund chart of accounts and post every financial event as a balanced JournalEntry.

FR-LEDGER-02

Posted JournalEntries shall be immutable; corrections shall only be made via reversing or contra entries, each referencing the entry it corrects.

FR-LEDGER-03

The system shall support configurable percentage-based allocation of a single contribution across multiple funds, with allocation changes applying prospectively only.

FR-LEDGER-04

The system shall run automated reconciliation between (a) payment-gateway settlement records, (b) bank statements, and (c) the internal ledger, surfacing exceptions rather than auto-resolving them.

FR-LEDGER-05

Fund balances shall always be derived from the sum of posted journal lines, never stored and independently editable.

12.4Obligation lifecycle, allocation, and grace-period taxonomy

GPT's analysis is the most precise of the six on three related points worth adopting verbatim rather than re-deriving.

Obligation status is its own field, distinct from any payment: Upcoming → Due → Paid / Partially paid / Overdue / Defaulted / Waived / Exempted / Written off / Cancelled. A late payment settles the obligation financially without rewriting whether it was originally on time — the obligation record keeps its original due date forever.

Payment allocation policy is configurable per tenant, matching the "arrears before current dues" pattern already found in AGAOSAS: oldest-obligation-first, newest-first, current-period-first, member-selected, admin-selected, or proportional across open obligations. Whichever policy applies, the allocation itself must be individually traceable per obligation line, not just as a lump payment.

"Grace period" is at least four different things that the source constitutions conflate under one term but that must be modelled separately: a joining grace period (before a new member owes anything at all), a payment grace period (extra days after a due date before default status kicks in), a benefit waiting period (before a member can claim, independent of payment status), and a reinstatement waiting period (after clearing a default, before benefits resume). UDS's 6-month rule is a benefit waiting period; AGAOSAS's 3-month probation is closer to a joining grace period with claim restrictions layered on top — treating them as the same configuration field would silently misrepresent one or the other for any tenant that needs both.

FR-LEDGER-06

The system shall model joining grace period, payment grace period, benefit waiting period, and reinstatement waiting period as four independent, separately configurable durations per benefit and per contribution plan.

FR-LEDGER-07

The system shall support configurable payment-allocation policies (oldest-first, newest-first, current-period-first, member-selected, admin-selected, proportional), applied consistently and each allocation independently traceable.

12.5Disbursement authorization & expense recording

Every one of the four source constitutions gates money leaving the organisation's bank account behind named joint signatories — typically Chairman + Treasurer + Patron, sometimes with a Secretary as an emergency fallback. Section 8.6's claim-approval chain covers benefit payouts; it does not cover general organisational spending — paying a vendor, moving money into an investment, a bulk transfer — which needs the same joint-signatory pattern but isn't a member claim. This is modelled as its own construct rather than stretched to fit inside claims.

A Disbursement Authorization requires N-of-M configured signatories to confirm before a payment instruction releases, mirroring the real-world bank-signatory pattern directly. An Income & Expense entry — a donation received, a grant, stationery bought, an AGM venue paid for — is categorised (transport, meetings, procurement, donations, grants, investment returns, …), attached to a fund, requires a supporting receipt, and posts through the same double-entry model as everything else in this section; anything above a tenant-configured threshold requires the same Disbursement Authorization as a bulk transfer.

FR-LEDGER-08

The system shall support a Disbursement Authorization construct requiring a tenant-configured N-of-M set of designated signatories to confirm before any non-claim organisational payment (expense, transfer, investment) is released.

FR-LEDGER-09

The system shall support recording income and expense entries with category, purpose, amount, fund, supporting receipt, and payer/vendor, posting each as a balanced JournalEntry.

FR-LEDGER-10

Expense entries above a tenant-configured amount threshold shall require Disbursement Authorization sign-off before posting; entries below the threshold may follow a lighter single-approver path if the tenant so configures.

14Anti-abuse logic

Defaulter management & the "dumping" problem

"Dumping" — paying only when a benefit is imminently needed — is the one failure mode every mutual-aid scheme is structurally exposed to and none of the four source constitutions fully solves in writing. The platform should solve it computationally.

14.1Contribution Consistency Score

For each member, per contribution plan, maintain a rolling score derived from the ratio of periods paid on time to periods obligated since the later of (join date, plan start date). This is distinct from — and a superset of — the binary good-standing flag every source constitution implies:

consistency_score = periods_paid_on_time / periods_obligated
eligible_for_full_benefit = (
    tenure_months >= plan.min_tenure_months
    AND consecutive_missed_periods <= policy.max_consecutive_missed
    AND consistency_score >= policy.min_consistency_score   // e.g. 0.8
)

14.3Dumping-pattern detection

A claim filed within a configurable cooldown window (e.g. 30–90 days) of a member exiting Defaulter status is auto-flagged — not blocked — for mandatory committee-level review, with the member's full consistency history surfaced to the approver. This directly operationalises AGAOSAS's own written caution: "these incidents will need to be verified before donations are done to avoid future misunderstanding."

Two further mechanisms, each proposed by only one of the four independent analyses but consistent with the source constitutions, are folded in here rather than treated as separate features: first-claim-only refund forfeiture — a member who has ever received a benefit payout forfeits the favourable exit-refund percentage on later resignation (the UDS pattern, generalised); and notice-window gaming prevention — benefits that require advance notice (marriage, outdooring) are time-stamped at submission and a late submission is rejected or flagged, never silently backdated.

14.4Proportional/vesting payout (recommended extension)

Beyond what any source document specifies, the platform should offer tenants an optional payout-scaling rule: benefit amount scales with consistency score above a floor (e.g. 100% payout at ≥95% consistency, tapering to a configured floor at the minimum eligible score), rather than a strict binary gate. This gives committees a middle ground between "pay in full" and "refuse entirely" — closer to the discretionary spirit MoWH's severity-graded illness benefit already shows for a different axis (severity rather than consistency).

exit_refund = policy.exit_refund_fraction * total_contributions_paid

17AI capabilities

AI proposes, humans approve — always

Only one of the four real analyses (Claude-web) proposed AI-assisted onboarding; none proposed a member-facing assistant. Both are adopted here because they compound naturally on a rule-engine-first design without weakening it — provided the guardrail below is treated as non-negotiable.

Non-negotiable guardrail

No AI subsystem may be a stage in a disbursement approval chain, and none may write a financial rule, ledger entry, or membership status change directly. AI output is always a draft or a read-only observation that a human role must explicitly accept.

17.1Constitution intelligence (onboarding)

On uploading a constitution document, the system proposes a draft configuration — contribution plans, benefit rules, eligibility periods, penalties, approval chains — for the admin to review line by line before anything activates. This is also where the numeric-contradiction check from Section 8.1 lives: if the source text contains a defect like St. Peter's "Ten Ghana Cedis (GHc 5.00)," the extraction step must surface it as a warning, not silently resolve it.

17.2Welfare assistant (member-facing)

Members can ask natural-language questions — "How much do I owe?", "Am I eligible for the funeral benefit?", "Why was my claim rejected?" — answered strictly from the tenant's live rule configuration and the member's own authorised data. It never answers from general knowledge and never exposes another member's data.

17.3Admin intelligence (advisory)

Narrative insights layered over the reporting stack (Section 16) — collection trend commentary, default-pattern flags, liquidity risk, benefit-liability forecasts, anomalous-claim call-outs. Read-only and advisory; it surfaces a report, it never files one.

FR-AI-01

The system shall extract a proposed configuration from an uploaded constitution document and require explicit admin approval, item by item, before any part of it activates.

FR-AI-02

The extraction step shall flag internally contradictory or ambiguous figures in the source document rather than silently resolving them.

FR-AI-03

The member-facing assistant shall answer only from the member's own authorised data and the tenant's active rule configuration, and shall have no access to disbursement or rule-amendment actions.

FR-AI-04

No AI-generated output shall be permitted as an approval stage in any workflow; it may only flag a claim, transaction, or member for human review.

18Subscription & billing

Platform revenue, kept out of the welfare funds it serves

Only Grok and GPT raised this among the six analyses; v2.0 of this document left the pricing model as an open question. It's now a stakeholder decision, resolved below.

The hard constraint, independently stated by two of the six analyses in near-identical language: platform billing must never touch, commingle with, or appear inside a tenant's own welfare-fund ledger. The SaaS operator invoicing "UDS Staff Welfare Association" for platform access is a transaction between the platform and that organisation as a customer — it has nothing to do with the GHS 15/month its members owe the welfare fund, and the two must be structurally incapable of being confused in the data model. Everything below operates inside that constraint.

18.1Tier structure — decided

TierWho it's forWhat's included

Tiers combine two dimensions deliberately — member count and feature access — rather than picking one. A tier isn't just "how many members," it's "how many members and which capabilities are unlocked," which is the standard shape for modern SaaS pricing and maps cleanly onto the real cost drivers: a 20-person alumni group costs almost nothing to serve; a 20,000-member federated university costs meaningfully more, in both infrastructure and support.

18.2Billing cadence — configurable, not hardcoded

Monthly, termly (aligning with school-year constitutions like St. Peter's), or annual — offered on every paid tier as a checkbox, not a fixed choice baked into the app. Annual prepayment carries a discount as the standard incentive. The actual prices for each tier and cadence live in a platform-operator settings screen, editable without a code deploy — the same "configuration, not hardcoding" discipline the welfare rules engine itself follows (Section 3.2), just applied to the platform's own pricing this time.

18.3Per-transaction pricing — considered and rejected as the primary model

Taking a cut of each contribution or payout was one of the three options originally on the table. It's rejected as the platform's core monetization lever: charging a percentage of the actual flow of welfare and bereavement money sits uncomfortably close to the wall this whole section exists to build, even with clean, separate ledgers — it ties platform revenue directly to the movement of members' money in a way flat subscription fees don't, and none of the four real-world constitutions studied have anything resembling it. If a transaction-based charge appears at all, it is restricted to a transparent pass-through of whatever fee the payment gateway itself charges — never a platform-added margin on top.

18.4Multi-campus billing — bundled into Enterprise, not a separate axis

Federation support (Section 7) is modelled in the data schema for every tenant, but its management screens and per-chapter fund isolation are feature-flagged and bundled into the Enterprise tier rather than priced as a standalone add-on. A university-scale organisation lands in Enterprise on member count anyway, so a fourth independent pricing dimension for "how many chapters" would add complexity without meaningfully changing what any real tenant pays.

  • Lifecycle: trial → active → past-due → suspended → cancelled, with a configurable grace period before suspension.
  • Feature entitlements gate higher-tier capabilities without ever gating a tenant's access to its own historical financial data — even on a lapsed subscription, a tenant must be able to export its own ledger.
  • Platform-owner visibility into billing status across all tenants, without visibility into any tenant's welfare-fund contents unless separately and explicitly authorised for support purposes.
FR-SUB-01

The platform shall maintain subscription billing (plans, invoices, trials, dunning) in a data domain structurally separate from any tenant's welfare-fund ledger (Section 12).

FR-SUB-02

A lapsed or suspended subscription shall never block a tenant's ability to export its own historical member and financial data.

FR-SUB-03

Feature entitlements shall be enforced at the application layer per subscription tier, without altering the underlying rule-engine schema a tenant has already configured.

FR-SUB-04

Tier definitions, prices, and billing cadences shall be editable by the platform operator through an admin screen, never hardcoded in application code.

FR-SUB-05

Any per-transaction charge shall be limited to a transparent pass-through of the payment gateway's own processing fee; the platform shall not levy its own percentage-based fee on tenant welfare-fund transactions.

FR-SUB-06

The multi-chapter Federation module shall be a feature entitlement bundled into the Enterprise tier, gated the same way as any other tier feature, rather than billed on a separate per-chapter pricing axis.

20Technology stack

Recommended stack

Optimised for a rule-engine-first product with strict financial-ledger integrity and a mobile-first, sometimes low-connectivity user base.

Nothing below was picked from abstract "industry best practice." Flutter, PostgreSQL, and shared-schema-with-row-level-security are here because all six independent AI analyses landed on them without seeing each other's work — the strongest evidence in Section 05's entire convergence map. A bespoke rule engine rather than a general BRMS reflects four of six explicitly rejecting the heavier option, on the grounds — visible directly in Section 4's comparative table — that the rule shapes across the four real constitutions are bounded and enumerable enough that a custom, auditable evaluator beats an opaque general-purpose one. 21Multi-tenancy decision

Isolation model

Recommendation: shared database, shared schema, tenant_id on every row, enforced by PostgreSQL row-level security — with an escape hatch to dedicated schemas or databases for the small number of very large tenants. This is the one architectural decision all four independent analyses reached without prompting each other (Section 05, row 1) — the strongest consensus signal in this entire document.

ModelFit for "thousands of small/medium groups"Fit for "a handful of very large employers"
Shared schema + tenant_id + RLSStrong — low per-tenant operational overhead, cheap to provisionAdequate with careful indexing/partitioning
Schema-per-tenantWeak — migration fan-out becomes unmanageable at thousands of tenantsStrong isolation, heavier ops cost
Database-per-tenantPoor — infeasible at this scaleStrongest isolation, justified only for regulatory-mandated separation

Given the brief's own framing — "thousands of independent groups with varying sizes" — the shared-schema-with-RLS model is the only one that keeps provisioning cost near-zero for the long tail while still giving each tenant provable isolation. Table partitioning by tenant_id on the largest tables (transactions, notifications) preserves performance headroom as a handful of tenants grow large, without requiring a different architecture for them — they can later be migrated to a dedicated schema if genuinely warranted, as an operational exception rather than a day-one branch in the codebase.

22Security & compliance

Controls

  • Tenant isolation: PostgreSQL RLS policies keyed on session-scoped tenant_id; every query path (including background jobs) must set that session variable before touching tenant tables — enforced by an automated test that fails CI if any query path is found missing it.
  • Financial approval integrity: disbursement instructions require the full configured approval chain to be satisfied server-side, re-validated at execution time (not just at UI submission time), closing the gap several source constitutions rely on human diligence to close (e.g. "Patron shall not withhold signature… unless compelling grounds" is a policy statement, not an enforced control, until the platform makes the chain a hard gate).
  • PII handling: field-level encryption for national ID numbers, medical evidence, and next-of-kin contact details; access logged per FR-AUD-02.
  • Payment data: PCI-DSS scope minimised via tokenisation through the aggregator; platform never stores raw card PAN/CVV.
  • Data protection: Ghana Data Protection Act 2012 compliance (registration as a data controller, lawful-basis tracking for member data, subject-access support) as the baseline regime; architecture kept regime-agnostic for future markets.
  • Auditor access: time-boxed, automatically revoked, read-only, export-logged.
  • Rule-change integrity: amendments require the same governance approval the tenant configured; the platform itself cannot silently alter a live financial rule outside that workflow, including for platform-owner support staff (privileged emergency access, if ever required, is itself logged and requires dual control).

22.1Fraud & anomaly signals

None of the four real analyses built this out; it appears only in the GPT checklist. Included here as deterministic rules to run at write-time, not as a machine-learning model — the architecture should leave room for one later, but nothing below requires it on day one:

  • Duplicate claims for the same benefit type and relationship within its occurrence cap.
  • A dependant added or changed shortly before a claim naming that dependant is filed.
  • A dormant, long-defaulting account suddenly clearing all arrears immediately before filing a claim (the Section 14.3 cooldown check).
  • An admin approving a transaction they themselves initiated, where the tenant has maker-checker enabled (Section 13.1).
  • Unusually frequent reversals or refunds against a single member or a single approver.
  • The same payment-instrument identity (mobile money number, card) funding multiple member accounts outside the declared proxy-payment flow (FR-PAY-05) — a proxy payment that explicitly names payer and beneficiary is expected and legitimate (a parent paying for their children, for instance); the same instrument quietly topping up accounts it never declares a relationship to is the actual signal.
